## Current official documentation

- [Chainlink VRF v2.5 supported networks](https://docs.chain.link/vrf/v2-5/supported-networks) lists Base mainnet and Base Sepolia coordinators and lane hashes. Base Sepolia remains the test network. The constructor accepts the reviewed coordinator/lane/subscription; no alternate test chain or invented VRF response is silently used in real mode.
- [VRF getting started](https://docs.chain.link/vrf/v2-5/getting-started) documents the request tuple, three confirmations in its example, subscription consumer enrollment, LINK/native billing and the extra-arguments selector. Pitlane implements the v2.5 subscription ABI with an immutable coordinator-only callback. It uses LINK billing and does not import the owner/migration functionality of `VRFConsumerBaseV2Plus`. This is a deliberately smaller adapter, not a claim to inherit that base class; review its exact ABI/callback against the deployed coordinator before release.
- [VRF security considerations](https://docs.chain.link/vrf/v2-5/security) inform request-ID binding, no bets after requests, no discretionary re-request, and a short callback that only stores entropy/deadline. Moves are committed before the request and betting closes even earlier. One fixed 24-hour oracle deadline permits a fee-free void; callbacks after it are ignored regardless of transaction ordering. This liveness tradeoff is explicit, with no reroll or alternate entropy fallback. Both reveals are checked against the original domain commitment.
- [Phaser introduction](https://docs.phaser.io/phaser/getting-started/what-is-phaser) and [Graphics API](https://docs.phaser.io/api-documentation/class/gameobjects-graphics): installed Phaser **4.1.0**, loaded only in the browser. Graphics, scaling, scene lifecycle and React cleanup provide a replay renderer; game outcomes are entirely independent of rendering.

The suggested MUD/Dojo framework was not introduced. The game uses a small auditable Solidity state machine on the required Base networks, rather than adding a separate world/indexer deployment or a Starknet dependency. Vercel AI SDK and the existing shared Jev adapter provide typed coaching and pre-submit gates.

## Game and economics

Three simultaneous rounds of strike/guard/feint. Guard beats strike, feint beats guard, strike beats feint. A win earns two points; a tie earns one each. Each round's VRF-derived arena sigil grants one bonus point to a fighter who chose that sigil. The highest total wins; equal totals draw. All three moves and a fresh 32-byte salt are bound to chain, contract, rules, match, side, fighter and frozen strategy. Salts/hidden moves are kept private until reveal.

Human trainers tune policy weights or coaching instructions. The AI coach proposes bounded weights; explicit human weights remain authoritative. Sixty deterministic practice bouts provide a benchmark, not a price forecast or claim of model learning. Registered strategy hashes and historical preimages are preserved. Pilots sample their frozen weighted policy autonomously, while Jev checks typed legality/fairness before commit/reveal. No arbitrary uploaded code executes.

Payouts are pari-mutuel. Winning backers recover principal and split **90% of losing stakes** pro rata; the winning trainer and house each receive **5% of losing stakes**. This does not take fees out of winning principal. Integer rounding dust goes to a winning backer. Draw, missing randomness, missing reveals, missing commitments or no winning backing produces a fee-free refund. A single missing reveal forfeits to the revealing side after its deadline. Trainers may back their own agents before betting closes, as requested by the canonical prompt; gas-only pilots cannot stake. Neither VRF nor commit-reveal proves honest trainer behavior or prevents collusion/Sybil betting.

New fighters require guardian enrollment before matches. Four concurrent matches, one active booking per fighter, 24 schedules/day, 24 VRF requests/day, 200 matches/deployment, 100 backers/match, 100 USDC per wallet/match and a 10,000-USDC pool ceiling bound exposure and execution cost. Owner controls cannot change winners, take escrow, change fee percentages or cancel selected unfavorable matches.
